## What phishing is

**Phishing** is a message that pretends to come from someone you trust so that you hand over something valuable: a password, a PIN, the code sent to your phone, money, or access to your computer. Most break-ins at schools and businesses start with one convincing email or text.

## Six signs to check

1. **Who really sent it?** Look at the address, not just the name. "GTBank Alerts" sending from `gtbank-secure-login.com` is not your bank. Watch for look-alike spellings such as `m1crosoft` or `micros0ft`.
2. **Urgency or fear.** "Your account will be blocked in 24 hours", "legal action". Pressure is meant to stop you thinking.
3. **Requests for secrets.** No real bank, school or company asks for your PIN, password or one-time code by email or SMS.
4. **Where the link goes.** The text of a link can say anything. On a computer, hover over it; on a phone, press and hold. If it goes somewhere other than the organisation's own site, do not open it.
5. **Unexpected attachments.** An invoice you never asked for, especially a `.zip` or a document asking you to "enable content", is a common way to deliver malware.
6. **Too good to be true.** Free data, a scholarship you did not apply for, a prize for a competition you never entered.

## Safe habits

- Go to the website yourself (type the address or use the app) instead of clicking the link in a message.
- Check an odd request by phone or in person, using a number you already have, not one in the message.
- Report phishing to your school's ICT unit, then delete it.

> **Important:** If you did click or reply, act fast: change the password on the real site, and call your bank's official number if you shared card or account details. Reporting quickly limits the damage.

## Not everything is phishing

Plenty of genuine emails have links: a teacher sharing a worksheet, a shop confirming your order. Genuine messages come from the organisation's real domain, match something you expected, and never ask for secrets. In the lab you will sort a whole inbox, safe and suspicious mixed together.
