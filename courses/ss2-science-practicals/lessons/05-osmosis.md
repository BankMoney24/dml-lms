## Osmosis

**Osmosis** is the movement of **water** molecules from a region of **higher water concentration** (a more dilute solution) to a region of **lower water concentration** (a more concentrated solution), through a **partially permeable membrane**.

Cell membranes are partially permeable: water passes through them easily, but sugar (sucrose) molecules do not.

## Three kinds of solution

Compared with the sap inside a plant cell, an outside solution can be:

| Solution | Compared with cell sap | Water moves | The tissue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hypotonic** | More dilute | Into the cells | Gains mass, becomes firm (turgid) |
| **Isotonic** | Same concentration | No net movement | Mass stays the same |
| **Hypertonic** | More concentrated | Out of the cells | Loses mass, becomes soft (flaccid) |

## The potato strip experiment

1. Cut equal strips from the same potato, blot them dry and weigh each one.
2. Put each strip in a sucrose solution of a different concentration, for example 0.0 M to 1.0 M.
3. After 30 minutes, take them out, blot them dry the same way and weigh them again.
4. Work out the **percentage change in mass** for each:

```text
% change = (final mass − initial mass) ÷ initial mass × 100
```

Percentage change is used, not change in grams, because the strips do not all start with the same mass.

## Reading the graph

Plot % change (up the side) against concentration (along the bottom). Points above the axis gained mass; points below lost mass. Where a smooth line through them **crosses zero**, the solution is **isotonic**: that concentration equals the concentration of the cell sap.

> **Note:** Blot every strip the same way before both weighings. Water left on the surface adds mass that did not come from osmosis.
