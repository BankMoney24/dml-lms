## What a titration does

A **titration** finds the concentration of one solution by reacting it with a solution whose concentration you already know. In this practical:

- the flask holds **25.0 cm³ of hydrochloric acid** of unknown concentration, with a few drops of **phenolphthalein** indicator;
- the burette holds **sodium hydroxide, 0.100 mol/dm³**.

```text
HCl  +  NaOH  →  NaCl  +  H₂O        (1 mole of acid reacts with 1 mole of alkali)
```

Phenolphthalein is colourless in acid and pink in alkali. The **end point** is the first drop that turns the whole flask a faint pink that stays when you swirl.

## Reading the burette

A burette reads **from the top down**: 0.00 cm³ at the top, 50.00 cm³ near the tap. Read the bottom of the curved surface (the meniscus) at eye level, to 2 decimal places.

```text
titre = final reading − initial reading
```

Example: initial 0.40 cm³, final 21.15 cm³, titre = 20.75 cm³.

## Doing it well

1. **Rough titration.** Add the alkali 1 cm³ at a time until the flask turns pink. This tells you roughly where the end point is, and usually overshoots.
2. **Accurate titrations.** Refill and start again. Add quickly until you are about 1 cm³ from the rough value, then add one drop at a time.
3. Repeat until two titres are **concordant**: they agree within 0.20 cm³. Average the concordant titres only; leave out the rough one.

## The calculation

With an average titre of 20.75 cm³:

1. Moles of NaOH = concentration × volume = 0.100 × 20.75 ÷ 1000 = 0.002075 mol.
2. The ratio is 1 : 1, so moles of HCl = 0.002075 mol.
3. Concentration of HCl = moles ÷ volume = 0.002075 ÷ (25.0 ÷ 1000) = **0.0830 mol/dm³**.

> **Tip:** Volumes in cm³ must be divided by 1000 to give dm³. Forgetting this is the most common mistake in titration calculations.

> **Warning:** In a real laboratory, wear eye protection: even dilute acids and alkalis can damage eyes. Use a pipette filler, never your mouth.
