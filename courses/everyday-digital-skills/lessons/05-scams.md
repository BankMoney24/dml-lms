Scammers rely on hurry, fear or greed. Once you know their tricks, most of them are easy to spot.

## The golden rules

- **Never share your PIN, password or one-time code (OTP)** with anyone, by phone, SMS, WhatsApp or in person. Your bank will never ask for them.
- **Hang up and call back** on the number printed on your card or on the bank's official website when someone calls "from the bank".
- **Slow down.** Any message that says you must act *right now* is a warning sign.

## Tricks to know

**The fake credit alert.** A "customer" shows you a screenshot or an SMS saying they have paid, and takes the goods. Fake SMS alerts are easy to make. Only trust money you can see in **your own** bank app or a proper alert from your bank, and check the balance before releasing goods.

**"I sent money to you by mistake."** Someone says they sent you money in error and begs you to send it back, sometimes to a different account. Often the money never arrived, or it came from a stolen account. Check your own balance, and if there really is a wrong payment, ask your bank to reverse it rather than sending money yourself.

**The account "upgrade" or "BVN update".** A message says your account will be blocked unless you click a link or give your BVN, card details or OTP. Banks do not do this by text. Do not click; call your bank.

**Too good to be true.** Investments that double your money in weeks, jobs that ask you to pay first, prizes for competitions you did not enter. If you must pay to receive, it is a scam.

**Fake links.** A message looks like it is from a bank, delivery company or government agency, with a link. The address may be almost right: a missing letter, an extra word. Type the real address yourself instead.

## If it happens to you

Act fast. Minutes matter.

1. Call your bank's official fraud number at once and ask them to block your account or card and trace the transfer.
2. Change your banking password and PIN.
3. Report it to the police and keep every message, number and receipt.
4. Warn people close to you: scammers often try the same trick on a whole family.

There is no shame in being targeted. Scammers do this all day; being quick to report is what counts.
