Customers decide in a second whether to look closer. A clear photo taken with any phone sells better than a dark, blurry one taken with an expensive camera.

## Light is everything

- Shoot in **daylight**, near a window or outside in the shade. Avoid harsh midday sun, which makes hard shadows.
- Turn off the flash for products; it makes shiny spots and flat colours.
- If the photo is dark, add light. Do not just turn up the brightness afterwards.

## Clean background

Put the product on a plain surface or in front of a plain wall or cloth. Remove clutter. White, light grey or a single colour that suits the product works well.

## Clean the lens

Wipe the camera lens with a soft cloth. Fingerprints are the most common cause of blurry, foggy photos.

## Hold steady and focus

Hold the phone with both hands, tap the screen on the product to focus, and wait a moment before taking the photo.

## Show what customers need to know

- One photo from the front, then the sides, the back and close-ups of details (stitching, texture, label).
- Something that shows **size**: the product in a hand, being worn, or next to a common object.
- For food, show a real portion as it will arrive.

## Be honest

Do not use photos you found online or edit away faults. A customer who gets what they saw comes back and tells others.

## Try it now

Take three photos of one thing you sell (or anything in your home): one in poor light, one by a window, and one by a window on a plain background. Compare them.
