Most people now pay and get paid by phone: through a bank app, a USSD code (the * and # codes) or a POS agent. This lesson is about doing it without costly mistakes.

## Three ways to send money

| Way | You need | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Bank app | A smartphone and data | Most transfers; shows the receiver's name and a receipt |
| USSD code | Any phone, no data | When you have no data or a basic phone |
| POS agent | Cash and the receiver's account number | When you have cash and no bank app |

Your bank publishes its own USSD code. Get it from the bank's official website, its app or a branch, not from a message someone sent you.

## Before you press send

1. **Check the name.** The app or USSD menu shows the name on the receiving account. If it is not the person or business you expect, stop.
2. **Check the amount.** One extra zero is easy to type and hard to get back.
3. **Check the bank.** Sending to the right number at the wrong bank goes to a stranger, or fails.

## After you send

- Keep the **receipt** or take a screenshot. It has the transaction reference.
- The receiver should see a **credit alert from their own bank**, not a screenshot from you. Ask the same when people pay you (see the next lesson).

## Set limits

Your bank app lets you set a **daily transfer limit**. Keep it at what you really need. If someone gets into your account, the limit is the most they can take in a day.

## Keep your PIN and phone safe

- Never write your PIN on your card or keep it in your phone's notes.
- Cover the keypad when you type your PIN at a POS or ATM.
- If your phone or SIM is lost or stolen, call your bank at once to block mobile banking, then call your network to block the SIM.

## Try it now

Open your bank app (or note your bank's official USSD code) and find:

1. where to see your transaction history;
2. where to set or check your daily limit;
3. the bank's official phone number for reporting fraud. Save it in your contacts.
