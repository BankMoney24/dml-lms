Your email, WhatsApp and bank accounts are worth a lot to criminals. Three habits keep them yours.

## 1. Strong, different passwords

A strong password is **long**. A short sentence only you would think of is easy to remember and very hard to guess, for example *my goat eats mango every friday*.

- Use a **different password for each important account**: email, bank, social media. If one website is hacked, the others stay safe.
- Never use your name, phone number, birthday or "password123".
- If remembering many is hard, a **password manager** app stores them safely behind one strong password. A notebook kept at home, away from your phone and bag, is also better than reusing one password everywhere.

## 2. Two-step verification

With two-step verification, someone who learns your password still cannot get in without a second thing: a code sent to your phone or shown in an app.

- **WhatsApp:** Settings → Account → **Two-step verification**. Choose a 6-digit PIN and add your email. This stops people taking over your WhatsApp with a stolen SMS code.
- **Gmail and Google:** myaccount.google.com → Security → **2-Step Verification**.
- **Facebook and Instagram:** Settings → Accounts Centre → Password and security → **Two-factor authentication**.
- **This learning platform:** your account menu → Your account → **Two-step sign-in**.

## 3. A way back in

Add a recovery **phone number** and **email address** to your important accounts and keep them up to date. If you are ever locked out, that is how you get back in.

## Warning signs

- A code arrives that you did not ask for: someone may be trying to get into your account. Do not share it with anyone who calls about it.
- You are logged out unexpectedly, or friends get odd messages "from you".

If this happens, change your password at once from a phone you trust, turn on two-step verification, and tell your contacts not to respond to strange requests.

2. Check that your email account has a recovery phone number.
