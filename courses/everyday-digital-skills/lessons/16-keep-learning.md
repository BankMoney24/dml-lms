You do not need to stop here. A lot of good learning is free, if you know where to look and what to avoid.

## Good places to start

- **This school's courses.** Open **Courses** and choose **Everyday learning**, **Skills** or anything that interests you. Many courses can be read without paying.
- **Libraries and community centres** often have free Wi-Fi, computers and short trainings.
- **Official sources:** government agencies, banks and big companies publish free guides on their own websites about their services.

## Learning from videos

Video platforms are full of useful lessons, and of people selling get-rich-quick schemes. Prefer channels from known organisations or people who show their real work. Be wary of anyone who promises easy money, asks you to pay to join a group, or pushes "investments".

## Job adverts: warning signs

- You are asked to pay for a form, training, uniform or "processing" before you start.
- The pay is very high for very little work.
- The interview happens only by chat, and they want your bank details or BVN early on.
- The email address is a free address (gmail, yahoo) for a company that should have its own.

Real employers do not charge you to get a job.

## Keep a learning habit

Ten minutes a day beats three hours once a month. Pick a regular time, such as after work or before bed, and set a small goal for each week. On this platform you can set a weekly goal and save lessons to read offline on the way to work.
