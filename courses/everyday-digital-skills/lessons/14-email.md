Many jobs, schools and organisations still use email for anything official. A clear email gets a faster, better answer.

## The parts of a good email

**Subject:** what the email is about, in a few words. "Application: Shop Assistant, Ikeja branch" is better than "Hello" or "Job".

**Greeting:** "Dear Mrs Okafor," if you know the name, otherwise "Dear Hiring Manager," or "Good morning,".

**Body:** say why you are writing in the first sentence, then the details, then what you would like to happen. Short paragraphs. No text-message spelling.

**Ending:** "Kind regards," or "Yours sincerely,", then your full name and phone number.

## An example

> **Subject:** Application: Shop Assistant, Ikeja branch
>
> Dear Hiring Manager,
>
> I would like to apply for the Shop Assistant position advertised on your website. I have two years' experience selling phones and accessories and I am comfortable with POS and stock records.
>
> My CV is attached. I am available for an interview any weekday.
>
> Kind regards,
>
> **Chidinma Eze**, 0803 000 0000

## Attachments

- Send documents as **PDF** so they look the same on every phone and computer.
- Name files clearly: *Chidinma-Eze-CV.pdf*, not *IMG_2045.pdf*.
- Check the attachment is really there before you press send.

## Your email address

Use an address with your name, such as *chidinma.eze@…*. Addresses like *sweetgirl4real@…* do not look serious to employers.

## Before you press send

Read it once more. Check the person's name, the spelling, the attachment and your phone number.

## Try it now

Write an email (you do not have to send it) asking a business for its opening hours and prices, with a clear subject, greeting, body and ending.
